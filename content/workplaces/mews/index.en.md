We are Mews colleagues in Czechia, organized together, and we work in the open. Even though we sit in different parts of the organization and work on different projects, we care about Mews. We want it to remain a community of great people and a remarkable place to work.

## Our vision and mission

The Mews union is created for and with people of Mews CZ. It is funded and operated independently from the employer. This gives us a safe space where we can talk openly and work together to shape the working conditions we believe are right for us.

An elected Workplace Executive Committee represents members in talks and negotiations with management. We engage in good faith-dialogue with management, challenge respectfully, and commit to the outcomes we reach together.

### What we aim to do

- Make sure our collective voices are heard before decisions that affect you are made, including being informed and consulted on layoffs, reorganizations, and changes to working conditions
- Negotiate a binding collective agreement that puts what we value in writing
- Bring our experience, concerns and ideas to management through constructive dialogue, then commit to the outcomes we reach together
- Build a supportive community across teams, especially in times of change
- Connect with unions/works councils already representing Mewsers in the other countries to exchange information and learn best practices

## How you can take part

### Who can join

Any individual contributor employed by Mews Systems s.r.o. can join, whatever your team, role or nationality, including colleagues on parental leave. The union is what you make it. Every member counts. Those in people management roles are considered on a case-by-case basis (see [FAQ for Mewsers](#faq-for-mewsers)).

### Joining takes 3 steps

1. Sign up through the [ICT Union application form]({{< relref path="register" >}}). Membership fee is typically 340 CZK/month, which keeps the union independent and covers union admin, training, and legal support.
2. Once approved, you will be added to the Mews union space on Signal, where we discuss, plan, and act together.
3. Choose your level of involvement (see [FAQ for Mewsers](#faq-for-mewsers))

### What you can do, starting today, no matter your membership status

- You can share with us what works at Mews, what doesn't, and what you would change.
- You can suggest topics for the collective agreement, member or not.
- You can talk with colleagues about why this matters and bring them into the conversation.
- You can help us run a topic, a survey, or a session with your team.
- You can ask us anything, even if you’re not ready to join yet.

## Talk to us!

## FAQ for Mewsers

{{< faq "Why did we form a union?" >}}
The only way to make change happen is to speak up and do something. While the idea of a union has always existed in casual conversation among long-time Mewsers, the recent reorg and layoff in July was the catalyst that got us thinking more seriously about how might we have a say in upcoming changes that affect us. Since then it has become a wider question: how can Mewsers in Czechia and the company communicate more effectively? These are the main reasons:

1. There is strength in numbers, and one person rarely changes an organization alone. Many of us may share similar concerns, but only some may raise it, and many not at all. A union lets us find what we have in common across Mews CZ and bring it to the company together through a recognized representative committee, which should also be easier for management than hearing it piece by piece.
2. Mewsers in other countries are already represented by unions or have stronger labor laws, ensuring that they are involved earlier in major decisions that change the organization and/or working conditions of the employees. Without one here, Czech colleagues are at a disadvantage and do not get to participate.
3. It's our right. Czech law lets employees form a union to protect their economic and social interests, without permission from the state or the employer ([Listina, Art. 27](https://businesscenter.podnikatel.cz/pravo/zakony/listina-zakladnich-prav-a-svobod/f1471640/)). Once at least three of its members work for an employer, a union can act there and negotiate collective agreements ([Labor Code § 286](https://www.podnikatel.cz/zakony/zakon-c-262-2006-sb-zakonik-prace/zneni-20180601/f3055225/), [Act 2/1991](https://www.mpsv.cz/cms/documents/95e9b25c-fb13-761f-d6d6-06f0d35e3a26/kolektivni_vyjednavani.pdf)).

Underneath all of it is what we said in our cover letter: people are what make Mews great.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Is this some niche thing?" >}}
Not at all. We're not even the first in the Czech Republic. Tech employees at [Microsoft]({{< relref path="workplaces/microsoft" >}}), [SUSE]({{< relref path="workplaces/suse" >}}), [Red Hat]({{< relref path="workplaces/redhat" >}}), Expedia, and in [other workplaces]({{< relref path="workplaces" >}}) have already organized through the ICT Union. [Tech unions are growing abroad too](https://portside.org/2026-07-26/how-ai-may-drive-union-resistant-tech-workers-bargaining-table) as workers and companies figure out what the advent of AI means for the different aspects of our lives.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "What can a union do that I cannot do alone?" >}}
In addition to giving the employees of Mews CZ a recognized collective voice for communication with the company, the Czech Labor Code grants certain rights and obligations to workplace unions that individual employees do not get.

1. A union has the right to negotiate a collective, binding agreement with the company that lays out and maintains the working conditions that we value
2. In case of reorgs and layoffs, the Labor Code (§ 62) requires the company to tell the union in writing at least 30 days before notices go out, and to discuss the plan with the union. The union can also send its own statement to the labor office. Where there is no union or works council, the employer deals with each affected employee individually, which means some may end up worse off than others. We mention this because it's how the law works, not because we expect it to be needed.
3. The union grants us the right to take part in discussions before decisions are final, because change is constant and Mews has to keep adapting. The Labor Code (§ 287) already provides for employers to discuss things like changes in how work is organized with the union. Involving the people affected early usually makes transitions smoother for everyone affected.

It's hard for one person to shoulder all the effort required to change an organization, even when many colleagues care about the same thing. A union pools our ideas, time and resources, so a point raised by one person can become a shared position that we can work together towards.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Mews is remote-first and already generous. Why do we need a union?" >}}
Great working conditions need to be actively maintained. Full remote work, generous parental leave, and unlimited PTO are part of why many of us continue to choose Mews over a competing offer from another company. However, change is inevitable, and we can choose to be an active participant in the change when the time comes. The union not only lets us preserve the working conditions we value, but also provides the framework for how employees can be involved in the decision making process and help shape the final outcome.

Mews being remote-first also adds an extra dimension. With teams spread across countries, there isn’t a group representing what Czech Mewsers have in common, such as how benefits are set up here. We like working here, let’s keep it that way, and make it even better for Czech-based Mewsers along the way!
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Why a union and not a works council?" >}}
A Works Council (rada zaměstnanců) in the Czech law is a much weaker version of a union. It is missing the most important right, the right to bargain collectively, and nothing it agrees with the company is legally binding. The company can decide to ignore those deals at any time. On the other hand, a union can bargain collectively, and the collective agreement it reaches is binding.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Why ICT Union rather than our own union?" >}}
Joining through the sector union frees up our time from paperwork and bureaucracy, so we can spend more time focusing on the interests of the workers at Mews. The ICT Union provides shared structural and access to legal support for workplace unions, as well as a network of other tech workplace unions, both in Czech and English. Read more about the ICT Union in the [ICT Union FAQ]({{< relref path="faq" >}}).

The additional benefits as an individual member of the ICT Union include:

- Education and training – so that we can upskill ourselves on how to organize and act together in a healthy and effective way
- Workshops & events – you can also participate in workshops and events about the IT industry, labor laws, and meet other IT industry folks on community events
- Legal support – as ICT Union members, we can request help from their legal department who can provide access to consultations with a labor lawyer

The Mews Workplace Union has the autonomy to determine how it wants to operate and engage with management.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Will Mews know I'm a member?" >}}
Only if you choose to tell them. Union membership is protected personal information, like religion,   marital status or sexual orientation, and no one can force you to disclose it. The Czech Labor Code (§ 316(4)) forbids the employer from trying to find out whether you are a member, or even whether you intend to join. Neither ICT Union nor Mews Union will ever share members' names: unless you decide to be open about your membership or join the Executive Committee, it stays private.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Can I be treated differently or fired for joining?" >}}
No. The Labor Code prohibits discrimination based on trade union membership and activity, so Mews cannot treat you differently, hold back your opportunities or let you go because you joined. Members of the Workplace Executive Committee have additional protection: the employer must ask the union for prior consent before dismissing them, during their term and for one year afterwards. If you think something like this has happened to you, ICT Union members can get a free consultation with a union labor lawyer, and the ICT Union statutes give members the right to protection against discrimination through the union's bodies.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "How involved do I need to be as a member?" >}}
As much as you choose! We need both active and passive members.

- **Active members** help organize, talk to colleagues, or take on a topic they care about
- **Passive members** strengthens the union through their membership – the more of us there are, the stronger our collective voice. The stronger our collective voice, the better our collective agreement will be.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "I’m on maternity or parental leave, is it worth me joining?" >}}
Sure! Those on maternity or parental leave pay a reduced fee of 34 CZK/month. When your leave is over and in case your position ceases to exist in Mews, the Mews union can help in conversations about finding an alternative role in the company, or negotiating conditions around your departure. Even if you end up not working for Mews anymore, you can still stay as a member of the ICT Union.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "I’m in a people-management role, can I join the union?" >}}
People managers who are interested in joining the union can be considered on a case-by-case basis. Please reach out to the union representatives (see [Talk to us](#talk-to-us)) to open the conversation with us! The usual standards of privacy and anonymity apply.

A workers union is made up of coworkers with shared interest, working towards a common goal, and we want to make sure union to be a safe space for employees, where members can trust each other.
{{< /faq >}}
